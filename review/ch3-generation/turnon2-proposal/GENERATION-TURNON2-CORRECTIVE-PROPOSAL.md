## §3. Q-1 — options analysis

The mismatch has three independent variables: **when in the PT day the smoke runs** (scheduling), **how much the smoke asks of one day** (scope), and **what the gate's clock does with a park** (window law). A fourth variable — the guard's own parameters — is a code change and is treated separately. Each option is analyzed on the §2 arithmetic; §5 recommends the combination.

### Option A — start-time alignment (schedule the gate against the PT reset)

**A-pure**: lifts and Julie's submission land just after the 07:00Z reset (07:00Z = **2:00 a.m. ET**), so the smoke starts a completely fresh PT day (0 dispatches used) with the whole cap ahead of it.

- *What it buys*: the maximum same-day probability of completion. On a fresh day, Chapter 4's observed traffic would have needed its first 19 segments to total ≤ 88 dispatches to let the 20th start; they totaled **89**. Even perfectly aligned, this chapter on its observed distribution is a **coin flip that lands one dispatch short**. Alignment improves the odds; it does not change the structure.
- *What it does not fix*: the window mismatch persists in form. If the plan parks anyway, the releasing reset is the *next* 07:00Z — still after any 4-hour bound. A-pure alone leaves Q-1's second half (the bound cannot contain a park) untouched.
- *Human cost, stated honestly*: 2:00 a.m. ET is the middle of Julie's night. She is prone to insomnia and takes bedtime medication; a gate whose success depends on her tapping Generate at 2 a.m. is a bad design and an unreliable one — a missed tap leaves generation enabled inside a gate window with no submission (the no-indefinite-enabled predicate, gate-1 §2.8.4, would force a re-hold stand-down). **A-pure is rejected on both counts.**

**A-variant — morning submission on a young PT day (the usable form of A)**: the executor-side work (census, clearance confirmation, lifts) runs in the early morning ET; the lifts land at roughly **12:30–13:00Z (7:30–8:00 a.m. ET)**; Julie submits when she wakes, inside a named submission window ending **15:00Z (10:00 a.m. ET)**. At submission the PT day is ~8 hours old, but its dispatch counter is what matters, not its age: under the holds nothing generates, so the day's only consumption before Julie's tap is the parked plan's completion (§4, reserved at ≤12). The smoke chapter therefore starts with **≥76 dispatches of start-headroom** for its pre-final segments — the best achievable position short of the 2 a.m. form, at a humane hour.

- *Residual exposure*: 76 headroom against a Chapter-4-class chapter (first-19 = 89) still parks near the end. **A-variant alone does not resolve Q-1 either** — which is why it is recommended only in combination with Option C.
- *Deadline discipline*: the submission window is a named gate bound. If no submission arrives by 15:00Z, the gate stands down: the re-hold pair runs as the stand-down close (a gate act, not an emergency act), the gate ends HELD, and the GO's consumption is governed by the validity rule proposed at §6.4 / OQ-6. The system is never left enabled awaiting a tap indefinitely (§2.8.4 carried forward, §7).

The binding constraint is the **start of the final segment**: the first N−1 segments must total ≤ 88 dispatches (or ≤ 76 after §4's reserve R = 12 for the parked plan's completion, which shares the day). Rule: **(N−1)·w ≤ 88 − R**.

A reduced scope therefore means *choosing a smaller chapter*, not trimming one: the smoke target becomes whichever Forever Pucked chapter's fresh quote fits the table — a real constraint on Julie's choice, and chapter sizes are not known until the census quotes them. Note also that **Chapter 4 itself (20 eligible) fits no row of the table** — Option B alone could never have smoked the chapter the program actually cares about next.

It is retained in the recommendation in one limited form: an **advisory fit test at census** (§5), which informs Julie's chapter choice and the outer bound's sizing under Option C, without making fit a pass/fail condition the gate can die on.

### Option C — window redesign (make the gate's clock contain the park) — RECOMMENDED CORE

Replace the single 4-hour decision bound with a **two-tier bound** in which a quota park is a defined, in-bounds **WAIT state**, not an incompleteness the clock runs out on:

1. **Inner bound — unchanged**: T0 + 4 hours. If the plan completes inside it, classification proceeds exactly as gate-1 §2.8.1 (COMPLETE on the full evidence set; FAILED on a stall/trigger/evidence failure; the fault path per §6.5).
2. **WAIT entry**: if, at the inner bound, the plan's *only* obstruction is `parked_quota` with stopReason `provider_attempt_budget`, totals failed = 0, and every other predicate stands clean (containment NORMAL, reconcile ok, conflicts at baseline, no trigger fired, recorder discipline clean), the plan enters WAIT. The classification does **not** land at the inner bound; the gate record names the WAIT phase and its bound, satisfying the no-indefinite-enabled predicate (gate-1 §2.8.4(i) is extended by enumeration to include "the smoke in WAIT inside its outer bound").
3. **Outer bound**: the **first PT reset after the park + 4 hours** — the plan receives the same 4-hour working window on the fresh day that it received on the first. **Extension rule**: if the plan re-parks in WAIT, it earns exactly one further window (second reset + 4 hours) **only if its done-count increased during the preceding fresh-day window** — a progressing plan earns time; a plan that re-parks with zero progress does not. **Absolute cap: T0 + 52 hours**, beyond which the classification lands regardless.
4. **WAIT discipline**: the sliding 35-minute stall test is **suspended for the quota park specifically** (the park is explained by definition; a stall declaration against it would be a false reading). Every other protection stays live at full strength: the nine emergency triggers (§2.8.3 carried forward), the unavailable-source law (an UNKNOWN on a /v2-sourced trigger still blocks COMPLETE), foreign-generation detection, containment and reconcile checks. Observation continues read-only; once the plan is parked and the picture is frozen, the observer's cadence may relax (proposal: hourly checks during WAIT, returning to ~5-minute polling at the reset and on any movement).
5. **Exit from WAIT other than by completion**: any non-quota stop state (`parked_storage`, `halted_unknown`, any state other than the named quota park), any failed entry appearing, or any trigger firing exits WAIT immediately into the ordinary classification path — the event is judged on its own terms at that moment, not at the outer bound.
6. **Classification law under C, stated completely**:
   - **COMPLETE**: the gate-1 §2.7 evidence set, evaluated **whenever it first stands complete within the outer bound** — plan done N / failed 0, job complete, exactly N new bindings against the smoke baseline (§4.3), phase delta in the three-line form, recorder discipline clean. Park events inside the window are recorded as in-bounds events, never disqualifying. (The smoke's N, quote, and watermark arithmetic are the new chapter's own, fixed at its census.)
   - **FAILED**: gate-1 §2.8.1(b) with "the decision bound" read as **the outer bound** — incompleteness at the outer bound for any reason other than a verified fault; a stall declared and not recovered by the outer bound; a fired trigger; an unattainable evidence set; Julie withdrawing. The failure close is gate-1's, unchanged (re-hold pair, Julie's contemporaneous countermand right preserved, the incomplete plan left exactly as it stands).
   - **SUSPENDED-BY-FAULT**: §6.5 (the fault rule, conformed to the executed precedent).
7. *What C concedes, honestly*: the gate can now span ~22–46 hours end-to-end (morning submission → possible next-morning completion). That is a longer enabled window than gate 1 ever contemplated, and it is only acceptable because every protection except the park-specific stall test runs uninterrupted throughout, and because WAIT is a named, bounded phase — never an unmarked overrun (the exact defect the gate-1 R2 law exists to prevent).

### Option D — guard-parameter change (code path — NOT recommended now)

Raise the 100/day cap, or reduce the 12-slot cohort headroom, so chapters fit more easily. Analysis only:

- **The cap is not ours alone to raise**: the 100 figure in the guard (`PROVIDER_ATTEMPT_DAILY_LIMIT`, honestly labeled app-selected when it was built) sits at the same value as the provider's own daily request quota as Julie has verified it for her account (100 requests/day, day boundary midnight PT — the same boundary the guard's 07:00Z reset implements). Raising the app cap above the provider's quota would trade a clean, explainable park for raw provider refusals mid-chapter — worse evidence, worse failure shape, no gain.
- **The headroom has a real job**: the 12 slots bound one fresh occurrence's worst-case split tree. The observed maximum is 6 — on this sample, a headroom of 8 would have admitted segment 20 (89 + 8 = 97 ≤ 100). But the sample is 19 segments of one chapter of one book; shrinking a safety bound to fit a single observation is how guards stop guarding. A defensible headroom re-derivation needs the dispatch distribution of normal operations (which do not exist yet — this gate is what creates them).
- **Cost of the path**: any parameter change is a code change — its own spec, candidate build, test battery, review, and deploy gate, sequenced *before* the turn-on gate and consuming its own round of Julie's and Rowan's attention — to solve what §3-C solves in gate law alone. **Deferred, not dismissed**: §9 carries it as future design work for the normal-ops era, when real dispatch data exists to size it.

## §6. Q-2 — fault-clearance prerequisites for the new gate

Gate 1 asked nothing about platform health before it started; a fault arrived mid-gate and, because the plan was already parked, the two events compounded into a suspension. The new gate treats clearance as a **precondition with evidence**, in four parts.

### 6.1 The clearance set (the routes whose health is the question)

The 1101 signature faulted the **DO-backed** routes while `/health` and the Cloudflare control plane stayed readable. Clearance is therefore judged on the DO-backed subset, each required to return **HTTP 200 with its expected response shape** (a bare non-500 is not a pass):

- client `GET /v2/d6-plans` and `GET /v2/d6-plans/<the parked plan>` (the census's own plan reads);
- operator `GET /v2/d6-containment` and the `drive=1` diagnostics projection (both containment surfaces);
- operator `GET /v2/d6-diagnostics` for the parked plan (carries its phase accounting);
- client `GET /v2/generation/capabilities`; client `GET /v2/quota`; operator `GET /v2/d6-quota-reconcile`; client `GET /v2/conflicts`.

`/health` and the control-plane pair (cf-api queue GET; worker `/settings` GET) are read as well — they were never the faulted surfaces, and their agreement is part of the T-8 discipline — but **they cannot substitute** for any member of the DO-backed set.

### 6.2 The two-set rule

Clearance requires **two complete passes of the clearance set, separated in time**: (i) the gate's census read set itself, all-200 at every member; and (ii) a **confirmation set** — the same reads repeated **not less than 30 minutes after** the census set completed — again all-200. **The lifts may not begin until the confirmation set passes.** A single healthy snapshot proves nothing about a platform that faults in episodes; two snapshots half an hour apart, on top of §6.3's lookback, is the minimum pattern that distinguishes "cleared" from "between episodes" on the evidence this program actually has. (The 30-minute figure sits deliberately beside the program's standing ≥35-minute stall-window discipline; Rowan may set it — OQ-7's companion.)

### 6.3 The lookback (fault-free history on evidence that exists before the gate)

The clearance clock starts at the **last 1101-class failure observed by any admissible source**. Admissible sources are logs, not recollections:

- **The scheduled monitor's run log** (`fp-ch2-generation-monitor`, running every **20 minutes** against the same route family — plan, job, quota, bindings snapshot — with its log at `~/workspace/goals/opal-roundtable/hidden_files/fp-ch2-monitor-log.md`). It detected this fault from its first affected run (23:06 ET, Nov 6) and logged the signature continuously thereafter; it is a working tripwire on exactly the surfaces §6.1 names. Its limit is stated plainly: it was built to watch a retired Chapter 2 plan, it is post-deadline and log-only, and it samples four routes — which is why it supports a lookback but cannot replace §6.2's full sets.
- **The gate's own contemporaneous working log** (any Wren read performed for clearance purposes is written to it with its timestamp at the time it is taken; a reading not in a log does not count).

**Rule**: the census may not begin until **≥12 fault-free hours** have accumulated on the monitor's cadence — **≥36 consecutive clean runs, no sampling gap over 40 minutes** — since the last logged failure. Twelve hours is chosen against the observed record: the Nov 2 window's unobserved gap ran 11h59m, and the present fault had already run ≥6 hours at the last logged evidence; a shorter lookback would certify health inside the observed duration of a single episode. If the monitor job is retired or its log gaps, the fallback is equivalent evidence, not a waiver: Wren runs the clearance set on the same 20-minute cadence for 12 hours, logged to the gate's working file, before the census. (Lookback quantum is OQ-7.)

### 6.4 Stand-down, and what happens to the GO

If the clearance set fails at the census or at confirmation — or a fault is observed live at any point **before the lifts complete** — **the gate does not proceed**. This is not a new mechanism: a live fault makes the census predicates unreadable, which is a census STOP under the standing law (gate-1 §2.11 already provides "before the lifts, the gate simply stops and preserves state"). The stand-down consumes **zero counted acts**, ends trivially HELD (the holds were never lifted), and is reported to Julie the same day with the failing reads named.

What it does to the GO is a **proposal, not a decree** — Rowan rules, Julie adopts (OQ-6): the new gate's GO passage be drafted with a **validity window**: a GO consumed by a pre-act stand-down (fault, clearance failure, or the §3-A-variant no-submission expiry) **remains live for exactly one re-run of the same gate within 72 hours** of the stand-down, once clearance is re-established under §6.2–6.3; a second stand-down, or 72 hours elapsed, lapses it, and the next attempt needs a fresh GO. The purpose is human as much as procedural: nobody — Julie tapping at breakfast, Wren at the console — should feel pressure to proceed into a marginal platform window *because a GO was already given and might be wasted*. A GO that survives a stand-down removes that pressure by construction.

### 6.5 A mid-smoke fault under the new window law

A verified 1101-class fault **at any point after the lifts** — inner window or WAIT — follows gate 1's suspension path, **unchanged in substance**: the classification SUSPENDED-BY-FAULT lands, the verified re-hold pair runs as its closing acts, the gate ends HELD, and Julie's contemporaneous exception right (leave generation enabled for a bounded period she names, Wren responsible for the watch and the end report; at expiry without fresh direction the re-hold runs) is preserved exactly. The window redesign changes the treatment of **quota parks only**; it buys no tolerance for faults. One drafting clarification, flagged as a deliberate conforming change for Rowan's eye: gate-1 §2.8.1(c) located the fault classification "at the decision bound," but the executed gate in fact suspended **when the fault was verified and the exception window expired, before the bound** — and Rowan's ruling accepted that execution. The new package's text should say plainly what the precedent established: **a verified fault suspends on verification (plus the exception window), not at a bound.** During WAIT specifically, a fault additionally freezes nothing and forgives nothing: the /v2-sourced triggers evaluate UNKNOWN under the unavailable-source law, so no COMPLETE can land on post-fault evidence in any case.
