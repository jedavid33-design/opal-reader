## 1. OQ-3 — recorder expectations at resume

### 1.1 The question, verbatim (package §9)

> **OQ-3 — recorder expectations at resume.** If passes ran during the window/C3 period, `d6_drive_passes` exists at this gate's census (§4.0 item 11 adopts its state). Is a drive-pass row for this gate's own passes **required** closing evidence, or is the P-1..P-9 grant/accounting evidence sufficient with rows as corroboration? (The dual-failure blind spot, R-06, cuts both ways and this package takes no position.)

### 1.2 Branch and blocking scope

Package §9 attaches an express "(blocking…)" label to OQ-1 (all branches) and OQ-2 (GR-B only) and **no such label to OQ-3** — its blocking scope is therefore genuinely unstated in the package, and this disposition proposes the reading rather than asserting one: **OQ-3 does not block the gate opening in either branch** (nothing in the census or the lift sequence waits on it — §4.0 item 11 already governs the recorder's at-open state), but **it conditions the gate's closing**: until Rowan fixes the recorder's evidentiary weight in the closing set, the execution record cannot state what closes the gate as success, because §2.3.4 defines closure by "the poll set itself, preserved raw" and the poll set includes the P-6 recorder surface at every poll. Scope: **both branches identically** (the closure-evidence question is branch-invariant; §2.3.2 adopts §2.3.1's completion values for GR-B by reference).

### 1.3 What the record establishes today

- **Rows are already inside the closure evidence — as reads.** Package §2.3 makes P-6 (`GET /v2/d6-diagnostics?drive=1`: drive-pass rows, live `getAlarm()`, recorder health) a member of every complete poll, and provides that "a poll with a failed surface is a failed poll, not a negative observation." §2.3.4 closes success verification on "the poll set itself, preserved raw… No summary substitutes for the raw polls." What is unruled is not whether rows are *read* but whether a row is *required as satisfying evidence* — whether a gate whose accounting surfaces all read complete can fail to close because a row is missing.
- **Rows already carry negative weight.** Abort condition A-6 (package §2.3.3): "a drive-pass recorder row shows an error outcome attributable to this plan's pass that the protocol cannot classify inside the in-set values" is an abort. A bad row can hurt the gate today; a missing row's weight is the open point.
- **At the wake-verification point, the weight is already fixed — by GR-6, as corrected.** Correction §5 (accepted): an in-set grant transition without a recorder row is an **R-06 evidence discrepancy** — meaningful corroboration, never independent satisfaction; wake verification pre-lift requires the recorder-row route or an alternative verification route Rowan has approved in advance; on a discrepancy the executor lifts neither hold and returns for review. That ruling is scoped by its terms to GR-2's pre-lift verification. It does not by its terms fix the weight of rows for the **post-lift** GR-5 phase or for closing — extending it there is this disposition's proposal (§1.4), not a claim that GR-6 already did so.
- **The census predicate exists and is branch-invariant.** Package §4.0 item 11: the recorder's state is **adopted** from the restoration census (at the opening baseline the table was missing; the window/C3 period may have created it — and did); the census records the adopted state and verifies the live read matches it; "a missing table after a period in which passes are known to have run is a finding, not a detail" and a mismatch is a STOP-and-classify.
- **The window gave the recorder its largest live trial to date.** Facts (window record §§3.7, 5, 6, 8): the table stood `missing` at every pre-stimulus read and moved to `populated` at the S1 event; row **id 1** was written ~1.9 s after the stimulus dispatch; the chain then recorded **296 passes**, and at every completed check every row read had `stage` complete, `driveInvoked` true, error fields null, and `recorderHealth.recordingFailures` remained **0** throughout. During the second 1101 fault's unobserved interval the recorder kept writing — passes 245–296 ran inside it and were read retrospectively at +54h — so rows were written during a platform-fault interval and survived to be read after it. And in the W-4 period the recorder's silence was **corroborated silence**: passes stopped, no rows appeared, and every other surface froze in exactly the matching state (job rev 88, reserved 12, live grant `seg:87:full` — window record §6; tail log 2026-11-05T23:28Z, identical). Across 296 passes, ~21h30m of churn, a platform fault, and a delivery stall, the dual-failure blind spot **never manifested observably**: no read ever showed grant movement without rows, or rows without the state they describe.
- **The limit on that evidence.** Rowan's R1 limit on the S1 event — it "establishes successful wake and observed drive activity, not durable alarm-delivery reliability" — applies symmetrically to the recorder: 296 faithful recordings establish the recorder's behavior on the never-stopped, pre-C3 chain. The resume gate's passes will run on a chain that has been stopped (registration deleted), restored, and re-woken — a state with **no live precedent** (analysis §7, standing). §4.0 item 11's adopt-and-verify census treatment already embodies exactly that caution at gate open.

### 1.4 Proposed disposition — ANSWERABLE-BY-RULE

Proposed rule, for Rowan to accept, amend, or reject (four clauses):

1. **Pre-lift, unchanged:** the wake-verification pass at GR-2 (and the GR-3 no-probe branch's observed effect) requires the recorder-row route or a Rowan-approved alternative, exactly as corrected §3.1 / GR-6 already rule. OQ-3 neither weakens nor re-opens that.
2. **Completion is proven by the accounting surfaces, with rows as required corroboration.** The satisfying closing evidence for success remains the P-1..P-9 set at §2.3.1 step 3's values (occurrence `ready`, execution `committed`, plan totals {eligible 1, done 1, failed 0, blocked 0}, derived reserved returned to 0, exactly one new binding with the watermark at 1842) plus the GR-7 closing census — because those are the surfaces that carry the facts of completion; a recorder row cannot prove a binding exists, and making a row *satisfying* evidence for completion would invert the evidence: the gate would be hostage to the R-06 blind spot in the wrong direction, unable to close over a segment every accounting surface shows complete because a recording failed.
3. **A missing row post-lift is a discrepancy, not an abort and not silent corroboration.** If, during GR-5, grant movement in-set is observed without a corresponding recorder row, the executor records an **R-06 evidence discrepancy** in the gate record under that name, with both surfaces' raw reads; the protocol continues on the accounting surfaces (which govern the outcome), and the discrepancy is carried into the closing record for Rowan's execution review. This deliberately differs from the pre-lift path (GR-6: lift neither hold, return for review) — the asymmetry is principled and is stated for ruling: pre-lift, nothing is at risk and stopping costs the attempt nothing but time; post-lift, the segment's fate is decided by accounting facts the row does not carry, and aborting a completed-or-completing dispatch over a recorder gap would manufacture the harm the discrepancy discipline exists to prevent. If Rowan prefers the strict form — any row-less in-set transition, at any phase, returns for review before GR-6a — the package can carry either; the asymmetric form is proposed.
4. **Error rows keep their existing weight.** A-6 stands unchanged: a recorder row showing an error outcome attributable to this plan's pass that the protocol cannot classify in-set is an abort, at any phase. The rule is thus asymmetric in both directions by design: rows can defeat the gate (A-6) and their absence is classified (clause 3), but rows alone never *make* the gate's success — the accounting surfaces do.

**What would close OQ-3 without a rule:** nothing documentary — the question is a weight assignment, not a fact. No evidence arriving before the execution census can settle what weight Rowan gives a surface; that is why the disposition is a rule proposal rather than REMAINS OPEN.

### 1.5 Interactions

- **OQ-1 design as corrected:** clause 1 restates GR-6 in its own scope and changes nothing; clauses 2–3 extend the same classification vocabulary (R-06 discrepancy) into GR-5 under a different consequence, expressly, so the two uses cannot be conflated at execution.
- **OQ-2 / GR-B:** §2.3.2's completion values are §2.3.1 step 3's, so clauses 2–4 apply identically in GR-B; but the recorder rule fills **no** §2.3.2 expectation — GR-B's early-pass grant expectations remain OQ-2's, and GR-B remains blocked regardless of OQ-3's disposition.

## 2. OQ-4 — the D-value at this gate's wake

### 2.1 The question, verbatim (package §9)

> **OQ-4 — the D-value at this gate's wake.** Prompt delivery after the wake is expected under R5a's past-due disjunct (the pending watchdog row is long past due), but D remains unobservable until the wake and the B-family is not closed (B1 leading, not proven). ### 2.2 Branch and blocking scope

It is **branch-invariant** (the wake slot is the same step in GR-A and GR-B) and blocks neither branch's selection; it must be answered in the full readiness review because GR-2's sequence cannot be final while a reviewer might still add an observation step to it.

### 2.3 What the record establishes today

- **D is unobservable pre-wake by construction, and the B-family is open.** Package §5.3: prompt delivery is "expected — not proven (the D-value family, B1 leading / B2 / B3, is unobservable until the wake; the §2.3.4 sliding stall test is the control that absorbs this)." Analysis §7 carries the same: the D-value family is OQ-4's territory; the bound absorbs any D inside it; a D beyond it is a failure by design. Nothing since has closed the B-family, and this disposition does not close it.
- **The window supplied the record's only two D-adjacent measurements, and they point in opposite directions.** (i) Grant 2: the stimulus command dispatched 2026-11-03T23:26:13Z; recorder row 1 at 23:26:14.899Z — delivery in **~1.9 s** to a chain whose standing registration was past due, under R5a's disjunct, on a chain that had never been stopped (window record §5; accepted at R1 / GR-1 at exactly that strength: the precedent "supports this design but does not prove the post-stop/post-clear case"). (ii) The W-4: a registration standing past due reached **26h14m37s overdue at window close with no delivery at all**, root cause UNCONFIRMED, and Rowan R2 forbids presuming it "will either fire or remain permanently inert." Between ~2 s and >26 h, the record contains **no distribution and no bound** — GR-2's qualification already rules that the 15-minute figure is "not proof of an upper bound on alarm delivery latency."
- **The controls on either side of the lifts already exist and are ruled.** Pre-lift: the 15-minute failure-decision bound with complete paired polling at least every 5 minutes (GR-2, as corrected at correction §1) — whose expiry *is* the D measurement for this gate: the first observed effect timestamps the delivery, and its absence at expiry is the STOP. Post-lift: §2.3.4's sliding ≥35-minute no-progress stall test (Gate 4's durable ruling) keys its window to progress events, so a slow-but-real delivery after the lifts slides rather than breaches. GR-3 added one genuinely additional pre-lift observation to the design — the no-probe branch's fresh, independently observed drive effect — but that observation is a **substitute route** to wake verification, not an addition stacked on the probe route.
- **GR-2's ruling contains a sentence adjacent to this question — quoted, and not treated as its answer.** "No additional waiting period is required merely to extend the bound. However, the complete fresh census, restoration verification, and grant-state checks remain mandatory." That sentence rules on *waiting*; OQ-4 asks about *observation*; and R4 of the corrections-round-2 ruling forbids inferring OQ-4's answer from the correction round in any case. The confirmation remains Rowan's to give expressly, and this section asks for it.
- **A structural point, labeled as this disposition's argument (not a record fact):** a pre-lift observation requirement *aimed at D itself* is unsatisfiable by construction. At GR-0 the registration is null by design (package §4.0 item 4 — C3's verified stop deleted it; the restoration's `clear` does not re-arm); D is a property of the wake's delivery, and before the wake there is no D in existence to observe. Any additional pre-lift observation could only re-read the surfaces the GR-0 census already reads in full. The honest alternatives are therefore exactly two: rule that no additional pre-lift observation is required (the package's ask), with D absorbed by the ruled bounds on either side of the lifts — or impose an observation that cannot measure the quantity it targets.

### 2.4 Proposed disposition — ANSWERABLE-BY-RULE

Proposed rule, for Rowan to accept, amend, or reject: **no additional pre-lift observation is required** beyond (i) the complete GR-0 census (including the restoration verification and grant-state reads GR-2's ruling keeps mandatory regardless of the bound's outcome), (ii) on the probe route, corrected §3.2's paired-poll observation under the ruled 15-minute bound, and (iii) on the no-probe route, GR-3's documented provenance **plus** fresh pre-lift observed effect. **What would close OQ-4 without a rule:** a ruling is the only closing instrument — no pre-census evidence can exist about a delivery that has not been woken. Classified ANSWERABLE-BY-RULE, not REMAINS OPEN, for that reason: the gate is not waiting on evidence; it is waiting on the confirmation the package asked for.

## 3. OQ-5 — post-completion chain behavior

### 3.1 The question, verbatim (package §9)

> **OQ-5 — post-completion chain behavior.** After GR-6a's re-hold, the alarm chain stands registered and the watchdog continues to fire into the halt (passes under the holds, the pre-window normal). ### 3.2 Branch and blocking scope

OQ-5 conditions the gate's **closing obligations** — what, if anything, is owed after GR-7 — in **both branches and on both end paths** (success GR-6a and abort §2.3.3). First check: TAIL-W4-CONTINUING, every marker at the closing baseline (tail log). The consequence for OQ-5 is direct: **by the time the resume gate can close, the tail watch will already have exited at C3** — the observation vehicle that has covered the chain since Nov 5 ends, by its own terms, two gates before this one. "Does anything watch the chain after this gate?" is therefore a real question in a way it was not when the package proposed census-sufficiency into a world that still had a standing window regime.
- **The state in question is fully observed and in-set, in both its forms.** After GR-6a the holds stand restored and the chain stands registered by its own re-arm cycle (its standing value is recorded at GR-7, "not predicted" — package §5). Passes under the holds churn grants in the §7.3 set — $0, dispatch-fenced, reserved pinned at the cohort figure — proven stable across 21h30m44s of observed window churn (window record §§5, 8). - **An asymmetry inside the package, flagged as found.** The package's **abort** path already assumes post-gate observation: §2.3.3 provides that if the chain stands revived and churning after an abort, "that state is **REVIVED-UNSTOPPED / OWNED** under the standing tail discipline until a future authorized disposition — named in the execution record, never silently absorbed." The package's **success** path, via OQ-5's proposal, would owe nothing after the closing census. The same physical end state — a registered chain under restored holds — thus carries an observation duty if the gate failed and none if it succeeded. No principle in the record supports that difference; it is an artifact of the abort text being written against the window regime and the OQ-5 proposal being written before the tail regime's exit conditions existed.

### 3.4 Proposed disposition — ANSWERABLE-BY-RULE

Proposed rule, for Rowan to accept, amend, or reject:

1. **The closing census suffices as the gate's closing evidence.** GR-7's full read set, with its enumerated-deltas discipline (§5 GR-7; §7.6 — any other delta is classified before the gate closes as PASS), is the complete evidentiary basis on which the gate closes. 3. **The §2.3.3 abort-path sentence is then conformed to name the same duty** (it currently says "the standing tail discipline," which by gate time will have exited at C3), so both end paths cite one instrument.

**The alternative, with its cost stated:** if Rowan rules census-only, §2.3.3's abort-path sentence must be conformed the other way — struck or rewritten — because the package may not promise on the failure path an observation regime its success path disclaims and no standing instrument provides. **What would close OQ-5 without a rule:** nothing evidentiary — the chain's post-gate behavior will be whatever it is, and both of its observed forms are already classified. The question is purely what observation duty the gate owes, which is a ruling.

## 4. OQ-6 — completion timing bound

### 4.1 The question, verbatim (package §9)

> **OQ-6 — completion timing bound.** No record bounds how long one segment's dispatch + attempts may legitimately take; §2.3.4 therefore keys the post-dispatch phase to the same sliding ≥35-minute no-progress test rather than a deadline. Rowan is asked to rule on whether a distinct, longer bound is required for the post-dispatch phase.

### 4.2 Branch and blocking scope

OQ-6 conditions **GR-5's post-dispatch phase** — the second application of §2.3.4's stall test — in both branches (§2.3.2 adopts §2.3.1's completion values, and with them the same test). It blocks neither branch's opening; §2.3.4 already operates on the sliding test and names OQ-6 as its open dependency, so the question is whether a deadline must be *added* before the readiness PASS, not whether the phase can be described.

### 4.3 What the record establishes today

- **The package's premise is still true, and no new evidence has arrived.** "No record bounds how long one segment's dispatch + attempts may legitimately take" was true at drafting and remains true: since the package, the window ran 296 passes with **dispatched 0 throughout** (window record §§5–6) — the churn never dispatched, so the post-package record contains **zero** new dispatch-duration observations. OQ-6 is the one question of the four on which the window and the rulings added nothing, and this disposition says so plainly rather than dressing the rule proposal as evidence-driven.
- **What the record does contain is attempt-count and cost shape, not duration.** Gate 4 (record-cited in the package's lineage): 15 segments completed, 76 attempts in total, settled $0.297 against a $0.294 quote — realized attempts per segment varied (including 23 `policy_block` outcomes across the set). Chapter 2's 18 segments completed within one run. None of these records a per-segment duration bound, and none is offered as one.
- **The attempt count for this segment is mechanically bounded even though its duration is not.** The segment is 569 chars, expected cost 8,022 units ≈ $0.008 (package §5.4). The cohort reservation for the single grant is **12 slots** against the 100/day provider-attempt cap (package §5.4; census headroom ≥ 12, §4.0 item 6) — the reservation mechanics bound how many attempts can be in flight against this occurrence. Recursive `POLICY_BLOCK` splitting without voice substitution (feature law F3) means a single segment's attempt chain can legitimately lengthen — Gate 4's 23 policy-block outcomes are the observed shape of that — so a short deadline would risk aborting legitimate progress, which is exactly the failure mode the sliding test was designed (Gate 4's ruling) to avoid.
- **The sliding test and a deadline detect different failures — the distinction that decides the question, stated as this disposition's argument.** §2.3.4's test detects **absence of progress**: two consecutive complete polls ≥ 35 minutes apart with no progress event (grant appearance or state/revision change; an attempt recorded for this occurrence; plan totals change; occurrence state change; dispatched increment for this grant) declare the stall, and any progress event slides the window. A deadline would additionally detect **slowness despite progress**. Every stall in the record — Phase B attempt 3 (no grant, no progress, declared on this test's precedent shape), the Phase A cohort stall, the W-4 — was a **no-progress** stall; the record contains no instance of progress-tokens-forever without completion. And the one hypothetical a deadline would catch — an endless splitting chain that keeps recording attempts — is already bounded on two non-clock axes: in **count** by the 12-slot reservation, and in **cost** by abort A-3 (the forecast line, 12,808 units settled delta, crossed before completion = forecast deviation + STOP) and A-4 (the detection threshold, 25,000 units), which the splitting chain spends against as it goes. An endless chain aborts on A-3 without any clock.
- **What a deadline would have to be set from.** A distinct longer bound could only be derived from provider-latency evidence — per-attempt durations under this provider, model, and splitting behavior — and the record contains no such measurement series. Any number proposed here (two hours, six hours) would be invention, and this disposition invents none.

### 4.4 Proposed disposition — ANSWERABLE-BY-RULE

Proposed rule, for Rowan to accept, amend, or reject: **no distinct longer bound is required for the post-dispatch phase.** The sliding ≥35-minute no-progress test (§2.3.4) remains the sole timing control in both the pre-dispatch and post-dispatch phases; progress events of §2.3.4's enumerated kinds slide the window **without limit of count**; the post-dispatch phase is backstopped on its non-clock axes by A-3 (forecast line), A-4 (detection threshold), and the 12-slot cohort reservation. If Rowan nonetheless requires a hard outer bound, the proposed route is that she set it **expressly at the readiness review, with its basis stated in the ruling** — an act of judgment on latency tolerance, recorded as such — rather than accepting any figure derived in this document, because none can be.

**What would close OQ-6 without a rule:** a measured per-attempt duration series for this provider path (which only a future generation gate's execution record can produce — i.e., evidence that cannot exist before this gate executes, and that this gate's own record will in fact create for its successors).
